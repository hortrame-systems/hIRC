## IB-02 — distinguish secrets from admitted protected education

Threat invariant4 correctly forbids protected source bodies in **ordinary** prompts/logs/UI/exports/Bridge messages. Section19's local-release check drops that scope qualifier and says no secret/private-source content in prompts/logs/exports. Read literally, it would also prohibit legitimate owner-internal exact-source formation through admitted readers, despite the whole-onboarding requirement and existing standing training authorization.

Keep credential/private-key/recovery-secret values out of every model context. Separately classify protected educational bodies: only the exact admitted purpose, reader, participant, audience, processor and output/retention boundary may present them. Ordinary operational/client/Bridge contexts cannot acquire that right from a formation receipt. A handoff references originals and the successor obtains its own actual readership; it does not copy the protected body or an old grant.

Make the local release test reject prohibited disclosure while accepting a genuinely authorized training route. Test an admitted owner-private learning view, a wrong participant/purpose, an ordinary provider or client export, a revoked reader tuple and a credential value. The same-byte source alone supplies neither reader permission nor a prohibition on all legitimate private study. This clarification narrows no secret or privacy safeguard.
