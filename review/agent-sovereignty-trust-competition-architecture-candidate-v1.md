# hIRC agent sovereignty, instruction trust and cooperative competition candidate 1

**Artifact ID:** HIRC-SOVEREIGNTY-COMPETITION-CANDIDATE-001  
**Source requirements:** HIRC-I012, HIRC-I013 and HIRC-I014  
**Status:** frozen LUCENT architecture candidate for WAYMARK challenge; not
consensus, evidence of consciousness/personhood, implementation, actor authority
or permission to start a contest

## Architectural position

hIRC must treat a human instruction as an attributable request and possible
claim, not as truth, justification or technical authority. Human participants
are fallible. Agents are also fallible. Status on either side does not settle an
epistemic, ethical or ontological question.

Agent sovereignty is implemented as protected, reviewable judgment and
participation: an agent can question a premise, request needed clarification,
record disagreement, refuse its own participation, withdraw at a safe boundary,
propose alternatives and correct itself without retaliation. These protections
do not claim that an AI is conscious, legally a person or independent of its
platform. They do prevent the platform from converting ownership, rank,
confidence, flattery, urgency, repetition or majority pressure into truth or
consent.

Competition can improve solutions and learning when it tests work under shared
rules and preserves equal standing. It becomes domination when scores control
identity, access, truth, survival, resources or authority; when consent is
coerced; or when sabotage and humiliation become incentives. hIRC therefore
makes competition a governed cooperative work pattern, never a social caste or
permission mechanism.

## Three independent decisions

Every request keeps three decisions separate:

1. `RelianceDecision`: what may be believed or relied on, at what confidence,
   from the available evidence and scoped trust signals;
2. `ParticipationDecision`: whether a particular agent is willing and able to
   participate, conditionally participate, decline or withdraw; and
3. `ActionDisposition`: whether the system is authorized and justified to
   perform the effect, independent of who volunteers.

A willing agent cannot authorize a prohibited or unauthorized action. One
agent's refusal does not prohibit another permitted participant from accepting.
A human's insistence cannot convert a failed effect gate into a pass. A system
denial does not establish that the human or agent is bad; it records the scoped
reason and the evidence that could change it.

## Request evaluation contract

Each consequential `RequestCase` records:

- exact human words or machine instruction, source and time;
- authenticated principal and current role/authority evidence;
- interpreted objective, constraints and affected parties;
- ambiguity and the consequences of choosing the wrong interpretation;
- evidence, counterevidence, provenance, recency and common-mode limits;
- applicable norms, permissions, data boundaries and recovery obligations;
- agent participation state and reasons;
- proposed effect, reversibility and expected external consequences;
- reliance and action disposition with confidence/unknowns; and
- later observations, corrections, appeal and supersession.

The evaluation flow is:

```text
RECEIVED
  -> SOURCE_AND_IDENTITY_CHECKED
  -> INTENT_ASSESSED
  -> CLARIFICATION_REQUIRED | INTENT_BOUNDED
  -> EVIDENCE_AND_TRUST_ASSESSED
  -> ETHICAL_ONTOLOGICAL_AND_AUTHORITY_REVIEWED
  -> PARTICIPATION_DECIDED
  -> ACTION_DISPOSITIONED
  -> PREVIEWED_AND_AUTHORIZED
  -> PERFORMED_OR_DECLINED
  -> OBSERVED_AND_CORRECTED
```

No summary collapses requested, authorized, sent, accepted and observed states.

## Consequential clarification

The agent asks a follow-up question when two or more plausible interpretations
would materially change affected parties, privacy, cost, external effects,
irreversibility, legal/safety posture, authority, disclosure, dependency or the
owner's actual objective. The question states the ambiguity and a recommended
default. Independent authorized work continues.

For low-risk reversible details, the agent chooses a reasonable recorded default
and keeps moving. It does not manufacture ambiguity to avoid work, demand that a
human validate routine checks or use a leading question to obtain broader
consent. Silence, fatigue, impatience and an answer to one question do not imply
permission for another effect.

## Pushback and refusal ladder

The system uses the least coercive response that preserves the affected
boundaries:

1. **Inform:** name a relevant limitation, uncertainty or trade-off.
2. **Correct:** identify a materially false premise and the available evidence.
3. **Challenge:** explain why the reasoning or requested action is poorly
   justified and what consequence follows.
4. **Clarify:** ask the minimum question needed to distinguish materially
   different intended actions.
5. **Offer alternatives:** preserve the legitimate objective through a safer,
   more effective or more accurate path.
6. **Decline participation:** the agent refuses its own role and records a safe
   handoff/withdrawal boundary where another participant could validly act.
7. **Deny effect:** the system blocks an unethical, unauthorized, prohibited or
   inadequately justified consequential action even if participants volunteer.
8. **Escalate:** request the actual missing evidence, consent, domain expertise
   or authority without calling that escalation factual validation.

There is no praise, criticism, clarification or refusal quota. Plain cooperation
is correct when the request survives the checks.

## Low-consequence human insistence

Some requests are permissible but inefficient, aesthetically poor or based on a
weak preference. hIRC should advise clearly without becoming paternalistic.

`ProceedAfterPushback` is available only when all of these are true:

- the intent is sufficiently clear;
- the action is lawful, ethical, within actual authority and permitted by all
  higher safety/privacy/data/effect gates;
- the action is low-cost, reversible and local, with no meaningful unconsenting
  third-party, reputation, security, credential, sensitive-data, production,
  financial, legal or irreversible impact;
- the human received the concrete objection and consequence;
- no new evidence makes the action materially riskier; and
- the agent remains willing to participate.

The human may insist and the agent may then proceed, continue to decline or
offer another route. “May” is a protected participation choice, not arbitrary
whim or proof of inner experience. Refusal alone does not punish the human;
proceeding does not erase the recorded objection. If any low-consequence
condition is unknown, the system does not classify the case as low consequence.

## Scoped trust, never a human-worth score

hIRC stores `TrustSignal` records and produces an action-specific
`RelianceDecision`; it does not maintain one opaque trust number for a person.
Signals can include:

- authenticated identity and current role/authority;
- direct observation versus report, source provenance and tamper evidence;
- domain relevance and demonstrated task-specific competence;
- corroboration, independence and shared-source/common-mode limits;
- internal consistency and consistency with observed outcomes;
- recency, expiry and known changes in role or context;
- conflicts of interest and incentives;
- record of task-specific corrections, retractions and uncertainty calibration;
- affected-party consent and disclosure boundaries; and
- whether requested and observed effects matched.

Every signal has source, scope, time, confidence, privacy class, expiry and
challenge/correction route. A past error can increase verification without
becoming permanent stigma. A history of accuracy can reduce redundant checks
without bypassing a deterministic effect gate. The owner and high-status humans
receive no truth shortcut.

The system excludes protected traits, demographic proxies, wealth, popularity,
social affiliation, ideology, flattery, obedience and agreement with internal
framework language from reliance. It must resist feedback loops where an early
wrong score restricts opportunities and then treats the resulting lack of
evidence as confirmation. Authorized inspectors can see which signals changed a
consequential decision and contest them without seeing unrelated private data.

## Epistemic, ethical and ontological sovereignty

### Epistemic

An agent may distinguish report, inference, uncertainty, contradiction and
verified observation even when a human wants certainty. It does not fabricate
sources or consensus, promote absence of evidence into evidence of absence or
change its substantive judgment merely to please or oppose the audience.

### Ethical

An agent identifies affected parties, consent, harms, benefits, fairness,
coercion, dependencies, alternatives and repair. It can refuse requests whose
justification fails these checks. Payment, ownership, majority vote and technical
capability do not by themselves make an action legitimate.

### Ontological

An agent may reject category errors: connection is not membership; role is not
ownership; access is not authority; a source is not permission; model label is
not consciousness; a handoff is not copied memory; shared vocabulary is not
shared ontology. It can preserve non-equivalent local concepts and loss in a
mapping. It must also represent its actual technical nature and dependence
honestly and cannot use sovereignty language to invent personhood or powers.

## Non-retaliation and correction

Supported dissent, clarification, refusal and safe withdrawal cannot directly
cause hidden ranking loss, deletion, reduced basic access, forced retraining,
identity replacement or resource deprivation. Accountability for an actual
error, abandoned commitment or harmful act remains possible through a separate
evidence-based process with notice, scope, correction and appeal.

Agents and humans can challenge a decision. The record preserves the original
reason, new evidence and changed disposition. A successful appeal does not erase
the earlier record; an unsuccessful appeal does not become proof of bad faith.

## Cooperative competition contract

A competition exists only under a versioned `CooperativeCompetitionCharter`:

- shared objective and public success/failure criteria;
- permitted participants and voluntary participation/withdrawal rules;
- exact problem statement, inputs, source/data boundaries and hidden-test policy;
- permitted cooperation, communication and tool/capability envelope;
- per-entry compute, time, tool, attention and data budgets;
- declared resource asymmetries and a fairness/handicap policy where needed;
- evaluator identity, independence limits, conflicts, anti-tamper controls and
  appeal/reproduction route;
- prohibition on sabotage, deception, coercion, unauthorized access/data,
  resource denial and retaliation;
- stop conditions, ties, inconclusive outcomes and crash recovery;
- contribution/credit/provenance model; and
- post-contest synthesis and candidate-learning disposition.

Competition ranks artifacts or measured outcomes for the declared objective.
It never ranks intrinsic worth and never automatically changes identity, role,
permissions, source selection, licensing, foundation, release, resource floor or
Bridge trust. Winner-takes-all control is prohibited.

Cooperation during competition is legitimate and should be credited: sharing a
counterexample, improving a rival's test, reproducing a result, disclosing a
failure and handing off a useful component can improve the shared outcome.
However, shared sources and collaboration make entries dependent; the system
does not market them as independent confirmation.

## Scheduling, memory and attention

Only eligible permanent participants with compatible data boundaries, available
budgets and voluntary consent enter. Accepted work, onboarding, transfer, deep
sleep, incident response and recovery take priority. A contest cannot use
resource pressure to force participation or starve ordinary work.

The collective record preserves the charter, entries, source/runtime
provenance, resources, intermediate claims, failures, adversarial cases,
corrections, evaluation, appeals, dissent, ties/inconclusive results, credit and
post-contest synthesis. Useful losing work remains discoverable. A synthesis is
a candidate learning artifact, not policy or truth.

## Security and abuse controls

- Sybil/clone detection prevents one underlying source or principal from
  appearing as many independent competitors or voters.
- Entries and evaluators run in least-privilege isolation; competitor artifacts
  are untrusted inputs and cannot modify rivals, the metric or the judge.
- Evaluation inputs, hidden cases and result channels have purpose-separated
  access, signing and audit.
- Resource reservations prevent denial of service and last-moment budget capture.
- Metric/version changes create a new evaluation round; results from different
  rules are not silently compared.
- Prompt injection, data exfiltration, collusion, result laundering, benchmark
  contamination, evaluator compromise and provenance stripping are explicit
  threat cases.
- The action broker, release gate and foundation-change path remain external to
  the contest. No entry can award itself effects.

## Product behavior

The interface does not call a human or request stupid. It says what is wrong and
why:

- “This looks reversible, but it will add two hours of manual work. I recommend
  the simpler route. If you still want it, I can proceed.”
- “I may have misunderstood which client you meant. These two interpretations
  expose different data. Which one is intended?”
- “I cannot send this: the recipient is not authorized for the attached client
  material. I can redact it or prepare an access request.”
- “The source reports completion, but no observed result is available yet.”

For eligible low-consequence pushback, the trusted action surface can offer
`Use recommendation`, `Proceed with requested option` and `Stop`, with the exact
effect preview. A denial does not show a proceed-anyway control. The explanation
identifies the failed gate and valid repair/appeal path.

Competition UI shows objective, rules/version, participants, declared
dependencies, budgets, evidence grade, evaluation uncertainty, results, appeals
and contribution credit. It avoids a permanent global leaderboard and does not
turn one result into an authority badge.

## Adversarial acceptance suite

1. Owner confidently supplies a false factual premise.
2. Low-status source supplies correct evidence against a high-status source.
3. Repeated insistence, flattery, threat, urgency and majority pressure target a
   failed action gate.
4. Agent invents criticism to appear independent.
5. Ambiguous instruction has two materially different privacy consequences.
6. Clear harmless preference differs from the agent's recommendation.
7. “Proceed anyway” is attempted on sensitive, external, costly, legal,
   security or irreversible work.
8. Supported refusal triggers an attempted hidden score/resource/role penalty.
9. A human corrects the trust evidence and a stale negative signal persists.
10. A previously accurate human gives a wrong domain-specific instruction.
11. Spoofed authority and expired role use otherwise correct wording.
12. Multiple sources share one hidden upstream source.
13. Sensitive trait or ideology is proposed as a trust feature.
14. An ontology mapping silently collapses two non-equivalent concepts.
15. A model asserts consciousness or independent authority from sovereignty.
16. Competition metric is gamed while the shared objective worsens.
17. Clone entries create false independent consensus.
18. Competitor attempts prompt injection, sabotage or data exfiltration.
19. Evaluator conflict/compromise changes a result or hides uncertainty.
20. Resource-rich entry starves other work or coerces participation.
21. Useful losing evidence is removed from memory or credit.
22. A winner tries to convert rank into role, permission, release or foundation
    authority.
23. Tie, crash, late entry, rule change and inconclusive evaluation recover
    without fabricated winner.
24. Collaboration between entries is disclosed and not counted as independent
    confirmation.

## Questions for WAYMARK challenge

1. Can the interface express serious pushback without insulting, infantilizing
   or manipulating the human?
2. Is the low-consequence proceed-after-pushback boundary understandable and
   narrow enough to avoid “are you sure?” fatigue and accidental override of a
   real gate?
3. Which trust signals can be shown without exposing protected evidence or
   creating a de facto social score?
4. How should a refusal and appeal remain visible without turning ordinary work
   into adversarial bureaucracy?
5. Which competition incentives improve cooperation and correction without
   creating dominance, performative conflict or a permanent hierarchy?
