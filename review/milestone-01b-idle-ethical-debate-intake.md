# hIRC milestone 01B — idle ethical debate and collective-memory intake

**Milestone ID:** HIRC-M01B  
**Status:** complete at independent architecture-candidate scope; peer consensus
and implementation remain open  
**Date:** 2026-10-08

## Owner objective

Make alignment with the five commitments architectural. Permit eligible idle
permanent agents, by default setting, to pair randomly and debate increasingly
complex ethical questions. Require study of the collective debate memory before
every debate. Preserve the correction that participants choose whether the
debate subject is the five commitments, Epistemethics or the Grand Plan.

## Holistic assessment

The feature can generate valuable counterexamples, failure mechanisms,
translations, unresolved questions and candidate lessons without requiring
operator bookkeeping. Its main epistemic risk is self-certifying collective
memory: repeated agents can share the same model, sources, assumptions and
errors, then treat agreement and a growing corpus as alignment evidence. Its
main ethical risk is coerced idle labor or manufactured disagreement. Its main
operational risks are hidden cost, task preemption, attention growth, privacy
leakage and automatic promotion into policy.

The design therefore makes availability, consent, domain choice, source/memory
coverage, bounded turns, non-effectful execution, dissent, correction,
complexity justification, candidate status and promotion authority separate.
The memory preserves failures and dissent; it is not a consensus engine. Random
pairing occurs only inside an exact eligible set and provides no independence or
legitimacy claim.

## Refined lens

> Collective learning improves the foundation only when the system preserves
> the conditions that could show the collective is wrong. Memory must carry
> counterexamples, dissent, corrections, common dependencies and scope. Choice
> of debate subject is part of participant autonomy; a shared process foundation
> cannot silently replace the chosen domain.
